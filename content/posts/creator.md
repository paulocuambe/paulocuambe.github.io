I've recently decided to write my own Operating System.

Needless to say, this is the hardest thing I have ever set myself to accomplish and one of the hardest projects anyone can choose to pursue regardless of background or industry experience.

A good command and understanding of computer architecture, hardware, compilers, linkers, debugging tools, assembly, virtualization, CPUs, memory and all the glue in between are your tools through this jungle. You need to be able to read hardware specs and ISAs, and translate that knowledge into code that achieves the user's intent.

The first act of building an OS is getting the computer to load your code and display some information on the screen. That alone is hard, very hard, and there are still many layers to uncover after that.

Anyone that says that this stuff is easy is lying to you, and you should not listen to them.

Operating systems are cool and hard to build -- that's why there are very few of them, but that's not what I want to talk with you about.

---

_Fasten your seatbelts. This is a rant about discovering my own stupidity and my disappointment with the education system._

## The best years of my life

I joined the Department of Mathematics and Informatics in February 2017 to study _Computer Science_. The next 4 years from that point on were all about learning and intentionally building skills to be useful in the job market.

I had so much fun. I learned about programming languages, mathematics, logic, databases, computer architecture, distributed systems, data structures and algorithms and a sea of other subjects.

I love learning; if I could be paid to just go on and learn esoteric subjects, I would take that job in a heartbeat, so Uni for me was incredible.

I can say without a doubt that those were some of the best years of my life. I made friends, I got to teach for a bit, I worked in teams with other people, I discovered my weaknesses and came out better than I entered.

If you were in the process of deciding whether or not you should go to university, I would say that you should in a heartbeat. No one should throw the chance of spending 4 years getting better out of the window. You will discover _worlds_ of knowledge and get to reflect on hard questions, which is a really important skill in life and in the workplace.

## Educating users vs creators

Let's make a nosedive and go back to the subject of Operating Systems I was talking about earlier.

Whilst on this journey, I once again went to a familiar place I had been to back in the days of university. I felt so dumb, so uneducated, so lost. I didn't even know where to begin.

Wait a minute! Why was that? Why am I clueless about where to start my OS building journey? Didn't I take an Operating Systems class where I scored at the top of the class? Didn't I ace my programming languages classes? I also aced computer architecture.

What is going on here? How can I be this lost? I swear to you that those were the best years of education I had up until that point!

I didn't know what an ISA was until this year, 2024, 4 years after completing all my classes.

> Eureka! Eureka!
> I was educated to be an advanced user.

Yeah, a user. Not a creator. Not a machine-bending expert. A user.

**However good my university program was, it was designed to make me a great user.** Someone capable of using programming languages, capable of writing performant database queries. It was not designed to make me a creator of those things.

---

Before my colleagues and teachers start throwing stones at me, indulge me for a few more minutes while I explain what I mean and why, in my opinion, the program was designed like this.

In the beautiful country of Mozambique, we have zero companies producing CPUs or companies working on operating systems, compilers or things at lower levels of abstraction. We are very far from that reality.

I work in the field, and I can say with certainty that even basic websites and CRUD apps are imported. 100% of the hardware we use is imported. As for the software part, probably close to 80/90% comes from companies outside the country.

In a country or reality like that, what do you need? A creator or a user?

Why would you be taught how to bend bits to your will when you are going to end up in an office to just click buttons and be a middleman the entire day? Why understand pipelining, branch-prediction, compiler frontends or backends? It _kind of_ makes sense.

## We must do better

I don't want to give you a history lesson, but all modern economies that are powerful were built on a strong engineering pedigree and first principles. This applies to all engineering and science disciplines.

At this rate, we are doomed to be consumers forever. We will continue to wait and rely on those who produce to share their excess with us.

## Conclusion

At this point, I am mad. I leave the conclusion up to you, and I invite you to reflect on this.

As for me, I want to be a freaking creator in this game. I want that power at my fingertips. I want to command bits.
