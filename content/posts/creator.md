I've recently decided write my own Operating System. Needles to say that this is the hardest thing i have ever set myself to accomplish and one the hardest projects anyone can choose to pursue regardless of background or industry experience.

Good command and understanding of computer architecture, hardware, compilers, linkers, debugging tools, assembly, virtualization, cpu, memory and all the glue in between are your tools through this jungle. You need to be able to read hardware specs, ISAs, and translate that knowledge into code that achieves the user's intent.

The first act of building an OS is getting the computer to load your code and displaying some information in the screen display, that alone is hard, very hard; and there are still many layers to uncover after that. Anyone that says that this stuff is easy is lying to you and you should not listen to them.

Operating sytems are cool and hard to build -- that's why there a very few of them, but that's not what I want to talk with you about.

## The best years of my life
I joined the Department of Mathematics and Informatics on february 2017 to study _Computer Science_. The next 4 years from that point on where all about learning and intentionally building skills to be useful in the job market.

I had so much fun, I learned about programming langues, mathematics, logic, databases, computer architecture, distributed systems, data structures and algorithms and a sea more of knowledge. I love learning, if I could be paid to just go on and learn exotheric subjects I would take that job in a hearbeat, so Uni for me was incredible.

If you were in the process of deciding whether or not you should go to university, I would say that you should in a heartbeat. No one should throw the chance of spending 4 years getting better out of the window. You will discover _worlds_ of knowledge and get to reflect on hard questions which is a really important skill in life and in the work-place.

I can say without a doubt, those were some of the best years of my life. I learned so much, I made friends, I got to teach for a bit, I worked in teams with other people, I discovered my weaknesses and came out better than I entered.

Ladies and gentlemen, please fasten your seatbelts. This is a rant about my stupidity and disappointement with the education system in my country.

## Educating users vs creators
Let's make a nose dive and go back to the subject of Operating Systems I was talking about earlier.

Whilst in this journey I once again went to a familiar place I was back in the days of university. I felt so dumb, so uneducated, so lost. I didn't even know from where to begin.

Wait wait!! Why was that? Why am I clueless about where to start my OS building journey? Didn't I have of Operating Systems where I scored at the top of the class? didn't I ace my programming languages classes? I also aced computer architecture. 

What is going on here? How can I be this lost? I swear to you that those were the years of education I had up until that point! How come I don't even know from where to begin? I was amongst the best in the class.

> Eureka! Eureka!
> I was educated to be an advanced user. 

Yeah, a user. Not a creator. Not a machine-bending expert. A user.

I didn't know what an ISA was up untill the this year, 2024, 4 years after completing all my classes.

**However good my university program was, it was designed to make me a great user.** Someone capable of using the using of programming languages, capable of using writing performant database queries. It was not designed to make me a creator of those things.

**I want to wield the power to create those things.**

Before my colleagues and teachers start throwing stones at me, let me indulge you for a few more minutes and explain what I mean and why the program was designed like this.

In the beautiful country of Mozambique, we have is zero companines producing CPUs or companies working on the operating systems, compilers or things at lower levels of abstraction. We are very far from that reality. It kind of makes sense.

I work in the field and I can say with certaint that even basic websites and crud apps are exported. 100% of the hardware we use is imported. As for the software part, probably close to 80/90% comes from external companies outside the country.

In a country or reality like that, what do you need? A creator or a user?

Why would you be thought to how to bend bits at your will when you are going to end up sitting in an office to click buttons the entire day? Why understand pipelining, branch-prediction, compiler frontends or backends. 

## We must do better
I don't want to give you a history lesson, but all modern economies that are powerfull were built on strong engineering pedigree and first principles. This applies to all engineering and science disciplines.

At this rate we are doomed to be consumers forever. We will continue to wait and rely on those who produce to share their excess with us.
