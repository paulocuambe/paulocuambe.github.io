I've recently decided to write my own Operating System.

This is the hardest project I've ever attempted.

Your tools in this jungle are: good understanding of computer architecture, hardware, compilers, linkers, debugging tools, assembly, virtualization, CPUs, memory and all the glue in between. You need to read hardware specs and ISAs, and translate that knowledge into code that achieves the user's intent.

The first step is getting the computer to load your code and display some information on the screen. That alone is hard, and there are still many layers to uncover.

---

_Fasten your seatbelts. This is a rant about discovering my own stupidity and my disappointment with the education system._

## The best years of my life

I joined the Department of Mathematics and Informatics in February 2017 to study _Computer Science_. The next 4 years were about learning and building skills for the job market.

I had so much fun. I learned about programming languages, mathematics, logic, databases, computer architecture, distributed systems, data structures and algorithms.

Those were some of the best years of my life. I made friends, I taught for a bit, I worked in teams, I discovered my weaknesses and came out better than I entered.

Universities allow you to discover _worlds_ of knowledge and reflect on hard questions for the entire time you are there, which is an important skill in life and in the workplace.

## Educating users vs creators

Building my OS brought back a familiar feeling from university. I felt so dumb, so uneducated, so lost. I didn't even know where to begin.

Why was that? Didn't I take an Operating Systems class where I scored at the top of the class? Didn't I ace my programming languages classes? I also aced computer architecture.

I didn't know what an ISA was until 2024, 4 years after completing all my classes. That was the extent of my ignorance.

> Eureka! Eureka!
> I was educated to be an advanced user.

Yeah, a user. Not a creator. Not a machine-bending expert. A user.

**However good my university program was, it was designed to make me a great user.** Someone capable of using programming languages, capable of writing performant database queries. It was not designed to make me a creator of those things.

---

In Mozambique, we have zero companies producing CPUs or working on operating systems, compilers or things at lower levels of abstraction.

I work in the field, and even basic websites and CRUD apps are imported. 100% of the hardware we use is imported. For software, probably 80/90% comes from companies outside the country.

In a reality like that, what do you need? A creator or a user?

Why would you be taught how to bend bits to your will when you will end up in an office clicking buttons and being a middleman all day? Why understand pipelining, branch-prediction, compiler frontends or backends? It _kind of_ makes sense.

## We must do better

All powerful modern economies were built on a strong engineering pedigree and first principles. This applies to all engineering and science disciplines.

At this rate, we are doomed to be consumers forever. We will continue to wait and rely on those who produce to share their excess with us.

## Conclusion

At this point, I am mad. I leave the conclusion up to you.

As for me, I want to be a freaking creator in this game. I want that power at my fingertips. I want to command bits.
